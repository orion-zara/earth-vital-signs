How the projection works

Each indicator is a simple trend plus an El Niño term. The trend is what the last decade of data supports. The El Niño term adds a lagged response to the annual Niño-3.4 anomaly, which comes from the slider above.

  • Temperature: 1.14 + 0.025 × (year − 2025) + 0.12 × lagged El Niño
  • CO₂ growth per year: 2.4 ppm + 0.7 × lagged El Niño, starting from about 427.0 ppm in 2025
  • Sea level rate per year: 0.45 cm + 0.25 × lagged El Niño
  • Arctic ice: drifts back toward its recent average, with no recovery trend.

Shaded ranges widen with distance from the last observation. Treat this as a scenario tool. It is not an official NASA forecast.

Making it more accurate

  • Backtest the coefficients against 1997–98, 2015–16 and 2023–24 and publish the error.
  • Replace the straight trend with an ensemble of regression, climate-model and persistence forecasts.
  • Feed in monthly NASA GISTEMP, NOAA CO₂ and Sentinel-6 sea level instead of annual values.
  • Add ocean heat content and Earth's energy imbalance, which are not charted here.
